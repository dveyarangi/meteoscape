# Discover

You are the worst-placed party to say what a thing you built is an instance of. Not for want
of knowledge — you hold a great deal — but because you are reaching for it while holding the
frame that produced the thing, so you retrieve what confirms the shape you already have.

This hands the shape to a process with none of that, and files what comes back.

**It returns material and never a verdict.** What it names is a candidate for your case, not
a finding about it. Why any of this: [EVIDENCE.md](./EVIDENCE.md).

## What it is for, and what it is not

**For:** you hold one account of something and it is the only one you have. You are about to
say what a thing *is*. You have one instance and are about to speak about the class.

**Not for:** ordinary recognition. Naming a thing correctly is not an error, and this does not
fire on plain naming. It fires where the existence you are ascribing has quietly become one
**exhausted by your way of grasping it** — where your handle on the thing has replaced it.

**Never the reason to change something.** A ladder always has a higher rung, so a pass will
always return room for improvement; that is the shape of the question rather than
information. Its output informs a change that already has a reason. A pass that motivates
work has inverted, and the honest name for that is worthening.

## Run it

**Describe the shape functionally, in nobody's vocabulary.** No local file names, no local
rule numbers, no local record names, no house terms. If the pass can only answer in your
words, it never left — and this is the whole of the method, so it is worth more care than the
rest of the tasking put together.

**Transmit the frame as little as the subject allows, and say what you transmitted.** The
capture that matters is not the vocabulary of your files; it is the framing you cannot help
sending, and a fresh process does not fix that by itself. A pass told what to look for will
find it and the finding will be worth nothing.

**Give it the rules it works under**, written into the tasking — they are the pass's own
instructions, not yours. Ask it for several families rather than one, each with where this sits on
that family's own ladder, what the family already knows about how this goes wrong, and its
confidence per claim.

**Ask it to name what your tasking smuggled in.** A first-class answer, and the one most
likely to be the useful part.

## Sort what comes back

**Recency for evidence, longevity for principles.** A claim that something *works* is
evidence and must come from the same substrate, recent enough to be about it. A claim about
how things of this shape behave in general is a principle, and there **longevity** qualifies —
which is continued life, not elapsed time: still cited, still taught, still used to decide
things. A dead old idea has age and no longevity. **The same source flips value depending on
which you asked it for**, and nothing about the source changes.

**Several doors, not one.** A family is a full alternative approach to the thing, not a slice
of it. So a disagreement between families is not a defect to resolve into one account — an
answer that harmonises them has probably lost both.

**Structural fit, not topical.** Retrieval is cued by surface features, so an unconstrained
*what is this like* returns subject-matter neighbours sharing no structure. Refuse a family
whose mapping cannot be stated.

**Stop where no further grounds appear.** Depth is a consequence of that, never a parameter.

## How to run it

**Spawn a separate process** — a general-purpose subagent — and tell it not to read this
repository. Reading it re-contaminates, which is the one thing the separate process buys.
Unless the shape is the repository itself: then the repository is the subject, and your
description of it is the frame. The pass reads it cold, from a fresh clone, and gets only the
question.

**Never feed one pass into the next.** A pass given an earlier pass's output retrieves what it
already said, and the second isolation buys nothing.

**Put the material into the work as it came back.** A summary written before you use it is your
reading of the pass, which is the contamination the pass exists to escape. It goes into the body of
the question, or the spec, that prompted it, the way `/impact`'s does.

## Neighbours

The two passes are a pair, and the overlap is intentional. [/impact](../impact/SKILL.md) is
the first pass: what a shape is made of here, and what depends on it — it needs the corpus.
This is the second: what the shape is an instance of elsewhere — it must not read the corpus.
Running both means two processes with opposite permissions, which is the point of having two.

[/align](../align/SKILL.md) stress-tests a shape with a person in the room; this runs without
one. A third pass, where a shape wants to go, is not built.

## Families
Each an alternative account of the shape — not a slice of one. For each: where this sits on that
family's own ladder, what the family already knows about how this goes wrong, and confidence per
claim.

## What the tasking smuggled in
The framing the pass could see in how it was asked.

## Nothing to take
Families refused for want of a statable mapping, and — when it applies — that the pass returned no
usable material. That is a result, not a failed run.
